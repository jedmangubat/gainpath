GainPath privacy

Last updated 28 September 2026 · applies to GainPath v2.9.0 and later

Your workouts stay on your device.

GainPath has no accounts and no ads. Your workouts, weights, body weight, notes and settings are never uploaded. The only data that leaves your device is listed below.

1. Stored on your device only

Everything you log is kept in this browser's own storage on your phone or computer: localStorage, plus a second copy in IndexedDB. GainPath's developer can't see it, and nobody else can either.

  • Backups are files you save yourself, wherever you choose. GainPath never uploads them.
  • Deleting it: Reset app at the bottom of Settings erases everything GainPath stored in this browser. So does clearing this website's data in your browser.
  • On an iPhone, Safari deletes a website's stored data if you don't open it for 7 days, unless GainPath is installed to your Home Screen. Keep backups.

2. Anonymous usage counts

To learn whether GainPath is useful, the app counts a few actions. It sends a message to GainPath's counter, a small service run on Cloudflare Workers, when you:

  • start a workout
  • finish a workout
  • set a personal record
  • save a custom program
  • create a monthly PDF report
  • export a backup

Each message contains exactly two things:

FieldWhat it is
idA random ID created on your device the first time the app runs (for example 097137a2-7d2c-41c0-a364-85c723e5c07e). It isn't linked to your name, email or device, and GainPath can't work out who you are from it.
eventWhich of the six actions above happened.

The counter keeps one record per random ID, holding:

  • when that ID was first and last seen
  • how many times each of the six actions happened
  • the times of the first two finished workouts, to measure whether people come back for a second one

It also keeps a monthly count of PDF reports. No workout contents, weights, names or emails are sent with these counts.

Like any request on the internet, each message reaches Cloudflare with your IP address and browser type. GainPath's counter doesn't store those. Cloudflare handles the connection under its own privacy policy.

Turning it off: there is currently no in-app switch for these counts. You can see your random ID in Settings → Privacy. If you want it deleted, send it with Settings → Send feedback or open a GitHub issue. Records are kept until deleted; nothing removes them automatically.

3. Only if you choose to send it

Feedback (Settings → Send feedback) is emailed to the developer through EmailJS. It contains:

  • the feedback type, subject and message you typed
  • the reply email, if you enter one
  • your name, sex, training split and experience level from your profile, to help understand the report

Native app updates (Settings → Native app updates): tapping “Notify me” stores the email address you enter, with the date, so you can be told when a native app ships. Choosing “Use a different email” adds the new address; it doesn't delete the old one. To remove your email, use Settings → Send feedback or a GitHub issue.

4. Other services the app loads from

  • GitHub Pages serves the app itself.
  • cdnjs.cloudflare.com serves the chart and PDF libraries.
  • cdn.jsdelivr.net serves the EmailJS library used by Feedback.

As with any website, these services see your IP address and browser type when the files load. GainPath sends them none of your data.

Fonts and icons are hosted with the app, not loaded from Google. The app sets no cookies.

Tapping a “Tutorial” link on a custom exercise opens a YouTube search, and Apple Watch sync runs a Shortcut on your own device. Neither happens unless you choose it.

5. Changes

Changes to this page are recorded in the changelog. GainPath is open source, so everything above can be checked in its code.
