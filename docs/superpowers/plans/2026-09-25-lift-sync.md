# Related-lift weight sync + single-source body weight

## Problem

1. **Related lifts don't inform each other.** `getAIEstimatedWeight()` never
   reads `ST.history`. It only uses the four onboarding baseline numbers
   (`CFG.keyLifts`) and a flat multiplier per *muscle group*. So an 80 kg
   barbell bench still gives a 10 kg dumbbell press, and a 60 kg lat
   pulldown turns into "48 kg per hand" for every back exercise, including
   dumbbell rows.
2. **Profile body weight is frozen at onboarding.** `CFG.bw` is written only
   by onboarding and Settings. Weigh-ins (`ST.bw`) never update it, and
   editing it in Settings never adds a weigh-in, so the two drift apart.

## Research basis

| Relationship | Evidence | Used |
|---|---|---|
| Dumbbell bench (both hands) vs barbell bench 1RM | Saeterbakken 2011: DB 17% lower (83%). Smoak 2023 (30 trained men): 75.6 vs 104.4 kg (72%) | 0.39 **per hand**, curved down for stronger lifters |
| Incline vs flat barbell | Elite: 21.5% lower at 25°. Trained men: flat 28.6% higher than 30° incline | 0.80 |
| Front vs back squat | Trained: ~78%. Typical: 75–85%. Back-squat specialists: 50–60% | 0.80 |
| OHP vs bench, row vs bench, deadlift vs squat | Coaching norms: OHP 55–75% of bench, row 80–105% of bench, DL 110–140% of squat | 0.65 / 0.85 / 1.2, **cross-pattern only as a fallback** |
| Rep→1RM formulas | Accurate at ≤10 reps. Error grows above that, and Epley overestimates | Reps capped at 12 when estimating |
| Transfer between variants | Smoak 2023: training either bench variant raised both 1RMs 10–14% | Supports treating variants as one strength pool |

## Model (why it isn't linear)

Estimates work in **estimated-1RM space**, not in working weights:

1. **Source strength.** For each related lift, take the best estimated 1RM
   from its last 3 sessions, using Epley `w·(1+(r+RIR)/30)`. Reps are capped
   at 12. RIR comes from that exercise's last-set rating (5+→5, 3–4→3.5,
   1–2→1.5, failure/unrated→0) and is applied to the final work set only.
   Old evidence decays 1%/week after 4 weeks, with a floor of 0.85.
2. **Pattern ratio.** Every free-weight exercise belongs to one movement
   family, with a ratio to that family's reference lift (the `LIFT_REL`
   table).
3. **Stability curve.** When converting between barbell and dumbbell, the
   dumbbell share shrinks for stronger lifters: `×(ref1RM/60kg)^-0.1`. This
   is the gap between the two studies' 83% and 72%.
4. **Confidence.** Same equipment ×0.95, other equipment ×0.90,
   isolation↔compound ×0.85, cross-pattern ×0.85.
5. **Back to a working weight** at the planned reps, leaving 3 reps in
   reserve on first exposure: `1RM/(1+(reps+3)/30)`. Then snap to the user's
   gear.

**Cables are a one-way, lower-confidence ballpark.** Stack numbers differ
between stations, so a cable lift is estimated from free weights (×0.75) or
other cables (×0.85). A cable number never feeds a free-weight estimate,
because with strongest-wins selection a heavy-numbered stack would inflate it.
Cable lifts never get a sync chip. The onboarding lat pulldown seeds cable
pulls only.

**Upper-body bodyweight moves are sources, scaled by body weight.** The load
is the share of body weight moved plus added weight, or minus assistance.
Push-up ≈64% of body weight (≈70% with feet elevated; Ebben 2011), and
push-ups train like a bench press at the same load (Calatayud 2015). Dips and
pull-ups ≈95%, inverted rows ≈60%, pike push-ups ≈70%. Each session uses the
weigh-in from its own date. Confidence is ×0.85. Only a logged weighted lift
retires the onboarding baseline, because push-ups capped at 12 reps would
understate a strong bencher. Lower-body bodyweight moves aren't sources: the
body moves in the barbell squat too, so bodyweight squat reps say nothing
about external load. Bodyweight moves are never targets.

Plate-loaded machines stay **excluded**. A machine's
number depends on its leverage and sled weight, so no ratio carries across
gyms. They keep the existing fallback.

## Which weight prevails (existing users)

- **An exercise's own history always sets its next weight.** It is never
  overwritten silently. This matches the existing "suggestions are
  Apply/Dismiss" rule.
- **Logged weights are lower bounds on strength.** Lifting 10 kg proves you
  can lift at least 10 kg, not that you can't lift 30. So among related
  lifts, the **strongest** implied estimate wins, and a lighter related lift
  never pulls a heavier one down.
- **The user's own rating beats the model.** If the lighter lift's last
  rating was 1–2 reps left or failure, it's a real weakness, and no sync
  suggestion appears.
- **Otherwise, show a sync chip** on the exercise screen when its own
  estimated 1RM is below 75% of what related lifts imply. Example: "Your Flat
  barbell bench press suggests ~27.5 kg here." It takes precedence over the
  small progression chip. Dismissing it is remembered per exercise, and it
  comes back only if the estimate rises another 10%+.
- The onboarding baseline acts as a dated seed. Once the real lift is
  logged, it's superseded. The ambiguous "Hack squat / Squat" and "Chest
  press" fields get an extra ×0.85. Lat pulldown only seeds Lat pulldown
  itself.

## Body weight

- `curBW()` returns the latest weigh-in by date, or `CFG.bw` when no
  weigh-ins exist.
- Logging or deleting a weigh-in sets `CFG.bw = curBW()`.
- Settings shows `curBW()`. Changing it there adds or updates today's
  weigh-in.
- Onboarding logs the first weigh-in.
- Loading or importing data re-syncs `CFG.bw`, which fixes existing users.
