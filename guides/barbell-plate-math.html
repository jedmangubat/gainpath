How to load a barbell: plate math

Subtract the bar, divide by two, then load each side with the biggest plates that fit.

Three steps

  1. Subtract the bar. A standard Olympic barbell is 20 kg (45 lb). Many gyms also have lighter bars, so check.
  2. Divide by two. That's the weight for each side.
  3. Load big to small. Take the biggest plate that fits, then the next, until the side adds up.

Examples

TargetPer sidePlates each side
100 kg40 kg25 + 15
60 kg20 kg20 (or 10 + 10)
82.5 kg31.25 kg25 + 5 + 1.25
225 lb90 lb45 + 45
135 lb45 lb45

When the numbers don't work

If your gym has no 1.25 kg plates, 82.5 kg can't be loaded on a 20 kg bar. The closest you can get is 80 or 85. That's why GainPath asks which plates and dumbbells your gym has (Settings → My gym). Every weight it suggests is rounded to one you can load: up when adding weight, down when easing back.

Machines

Plate-loaded machines like leg presses and hack squats have a sled that weighs something too, and it's different on every machine. Log the sled weight once, and the total stays honest.
