When should I increase the weight?

Go by how many reps you had left on your last set. With 3 or more left, add weight next time. With 1–2 left, stay. If you fail twice in a row, back off.

The rule

After your last set of an exercise, ask one question: how many more reps could I have done with good form? That number is your reps in reserve.

Reps left on the last setNext session
5 or moreAdd a full step
3–4Add the smallest step
1–2Keep the weight. This is where you want to be.
0, to failureKeep the weight. If it happens two sessions in a row, drop one step.

How big is a step?

This is what GainPath uses:

  • Full step: 5 kg (10 lb) for leg and back lifts like squats, deadlifts and rows. 2.5 kg (5 lb) for everything else.
  • Smallest step: 2.5 kg (5 lb). On dumbbells under 20 kg it's 1 kg (2 lb), because a jump from 8 to 10 kg is already a big change.

Then round to what you can actually load. If your gym's dumbbells go 12.5, 15, 17.5, a "+1 kg" becomes the next dumbbell up.

Examples

  • Squat 100 kg × 5, 5+ reps left → 105 kg next time.
  • Bench 80 kg × 8, 3–4 reps left → 82.5 kg.
  • Dumbbell press 14 kg × 10, 3–4 reps left → 15 kg.
  • Bench 80 kg to failure two sessions running → 77.5 kg.

Why not just add weight every week?

A fixed schedule works for beginners until it suddenly doesn't, and then you fail, repeat, and fail again. Rating each last set lets a lift that's moving fast keep moving, while one that's stuck gets held or eased back before it turns into a grind.
