Dumbbell vs barbell: how much should I lift?

Two dumbbells together usually add up to less than the barbell weight you can lift for the same movement. For the bench press, expect roughly 72–83% of your barbell weight, split between two hands.

Why dumbbells feel heavier

With a barbell, both arms push one bar, and the bar keeps your hands the same distance apart. With dumbbells, each arm balances its own weight on its own path. That extra stabilising work means you can't move as much total weight.

What the measurements say

  • Bench press: in a 2011 study (Saeterbakken and colleagues), the dumbbell one-rep max was about 17% lower than the barbell one, so about 83%. A 2023 study of 30 trained men (Smoak and colleagues) found about 72%. Divide by two for the weight in each hand.
  • Stronger lifters tend to land toward the lower end. Heavy dumbbells are harder to control, and the gap between those two studies' groups points the same way, though no study pins it down exactly.
  • Smith machine bench: a 2005 study (Cotterman and colleagues) found the guided bar a little lower than the free barbell, not higher, at roughly 87–89% at typical loads.

Worked example

Barbell bench one-rep max: 100 kg

Dumbbell bench one-rep max: about 72–83 kg total, so about 36–41 kg per hand.

For training sets you'd go lighter than that. For sets of 10 with about 3 reps in reserve you'd use roughly 70% of it, around 25–28 kg per hand. Then round to a dumbbell your gym actually has.

Going the other way

If you only know your dumbbell weight, the same ratios work in reverse: per-hand weight ÷ 0.36–0.41 ≈ barbell weight. Treat it as a starting point for the first set, not a target.
